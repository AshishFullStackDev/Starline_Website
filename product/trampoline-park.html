What is a Trampoline Park?

A Trampoline Park is a recreational activity area made up of interconnected or individual trampoline stations designed for jumping, recreational activities and selected trampoline-based attractions. It provides dynamic, high-energy entertainment suitable for children, teenagers, fitness enthusiasts, and family groups in commercial entertainment settings.

A commercial trampoline park can include:

  • Individual trampoline stations: Standalone circular or rectangular trampoline units designed for single-jumper recreation.
  • Multi-bay trampoline areas: Contiguous interconnected trampoline beds enabling continuous jumping across multiple interconnected zones.
  • Jumping mats: High-grade, UV-stabilized woven polypropylene jumping mats engineered for responsive bounce and tear resistance.
  • Steel support frames: Heavy-gauge galvanized tubular steel framing forming a rigid, stable sub-structure.
  • Safety padding: High-density, impact-absorbing foam padding wrapped in protective PVC covering all perimeter frame rails and springs.
  • Safety enclosure nets: High-tenacity mesh enclosure netting supported by padded steel uprights preventing jumpers from bouncing off-frame.
  • Entry/exit areas: Designated padded boarding zones, shoe cubbies, and gated access points for controlled visitor flow.
  • Operator/supervision areas: Elevated or clear-line supervision walkways allowing staff to monitor visitor conduct and safety.
  • Additional trampoline-based attractions: Specialized modular additions such as foam pits, basketball slam-dunk channels, or dodgeball arenas where applicable.

The final configuration depends on the project. Starline Adventures works closely with clients to configure the exact blend of stations and features suited to their available space and guest demographics.

Trampoline Park Design & Engineering

Engineering a commercial trampoline park requires deliberate spatial coordination to ensure participant safety, smooth guest turnover, and robust durability. Key factors evaluated during our design and engineering process include:

  • Available floor area: Exact survey of internal floor dimensions, ceiling heights, column positions, and obstacle clearances.
  • Number of trampoline stations: Calculating the optimal number of jump beds to meet client visitor capacity and commercial goals.
  • Modular layout: Modular steel grid layouts that facilitate phased installation, future expansion, or station reconfiguration.
  • Multi-bay configuration: Interlinked trampoline frames engineered with heavy cross-bracing to distribute dynamic jumping loads evenly.
  • Visitor circulation: Wide, unobstructed perimeter aisles allowing spectators, parents, and queueing participants to move safely.
  • Entry and exit: Dedicated check-in boarding points and exit gates preventing crowd congestion at active jumping perimeters.
  • Safety clearance: Ample vertical ceiling clearance and lateral buffer distances away from structural walls and pillars.
  • Structural support: Heavy-duty galvanized tubular framing designed for commercial dynamic impact cycles.
  • Padding and enclosure requirements: Seamless full-coverage padding over all springs and rigid steel posts to prevent entrapment.
  • Indoor/outdoor installation: Weatherproof materials and corrosion-resistant coatings engineered for outdoor parks or climate-controlled indoor arenas.
  • Maintenance access: Thoughtfully placed inspection hatches and removable padding sections for straightforward spring and frame checks.
  • Operator/supervision area: Centralized observation stations providing range staff with 360-degree line of sight across all jump stations.

Trampoline Park Size & Space Requirement

Because every commercial recreation site possesses different architectural characteristics, there is no single fixed minimum area for a trampoline park. Configurations can range from approximately 10 ft round trampoline units to larger multi-bay continuous arenas.

The required space depends on:

  • Number of trampoline stations: Total jumping beds required to satisfy expected hourly visitor throughput.
  • Overall park layout: Square, rectangular, or irregular building dimensions and internal pillar spacing.
  • Available floor area: Total usable carpet area dedicated to the trampoline attraction.
  • Visitor capacity: Peak-hour crowd expectations dictating queuing zones and jumper staging spaces.
  • Walkways: Safe circulation corridors around the trampoline perimeter for guests, staff, and spectators.
  • Safety clearances: Unobstructed vertical ceiling clearance and lateral clearance between trampoline frames and permanent walls.
  • Entry/exit: Designated access ramps, steps, and cubby areas for shoe storage and wristband check-in.
  • Operator area: Dedicated monitoring desks and first-aid/briefing stations.
  • Additional activities: Space for foam pits, basketball hoops, or seating if integrated into the facility.
  • Indoor/outdoor location: Weatherproofing envelopes, shade structures, or HVAC clearance requirements.

Starline Adventures can prepare a site-specific layout after reviewing the available area and required activity configuration.

Trampoline Park Safety Systems

Safety in a commercial trampoline facility is achieved through deliberate mechanical engineering, durable impact cushioning, and continuous supervision. Our trampoline-specific safety systems include:

  • High-density safety padding: 20 mm high-density, closed-cell foam padding covering all steel frame rails and spring assemblies to cushion inadvertent contact.
  • Safety enclosure netting: High-tenacity, small-mesh perimeter netting designed to keep jumpers securely within the jumping zone.
  • Steel enclosure uprights: Sturdy tubular steel support posts fitted with thick protective foam sleeves and durable vinyl jackets.
  • Properly installed jumping mats: Commercial-grade polypropylene mats reinforced with multi-row polyester webbing and secure steel V-rings.
  • Suitable springs: High-tensile carbon steel springs calibrated to deliver controlled, progressive deceleration during heavy bounce impacts.
  • Structural inspection: Factory verification of all frame welds, joints, and bolt connections prior to dispatch.
  • Equipment inspection: Comprehensive inspection of mat integrity, spring elasticity, and padding alignment post-assembly.
  • Controlled visitor access: Defined single-entry gates allowing staff to verify socks, waivers, and participant capacity limits.
  • Operator supervision: Direct line-of-sight supervision layouts allowing attendants to enforce one-jumper-per-mat rules where applicable.
  • Pre-use checks: Daily checklist protocols covering spring attachment, mat tension, and pad positioning prior to opening.
  • Regular maintenance: Scheduled replacement guidelines for high-wear parts like springs and netting to ensure consistent performance.
  • Replacement of worn components: Modular component design allowing quick changeout of springs, mats, or padding sections when required.

Trampoline Park Manufacturing Process

Our manufacturing process follows an organized, rigorous sequence to produce durable, commercial-grade trampoline systems:

  1. Project requirement discussion: Reviewing client goals, commercial model, target visitor capacity, and facility constraints.
  2. Site measurement/review: Evaluating floor plans, pillar locations, ceiling clearances, and electrical/HVAC lines.
  3. Layout and configuration planning: Drafting 2D and 3D arena layouts balancing jump stations, walkways, and safety zones.
  4. Structural design: Engineering steel frame thicknesses, modular bay linkages, and load-distribution footings.
  5. Material selection: Sourcing prime galvanized tubular steel, UV-stabilized polypropylene mats, and high-density foam.
  6. Frame fabrication: Precision cutting, bending, drilling, and welding of tubular steel frame components.
  7. Surface treatment: Applying anti-corrosive powder coating or hot-dip galvanizing for long-term corrosion resistance.
  8. Spring and jumping-mat integration: Calibrating spring dimensions and test-fitting jumping mats on prototype frames.
  9. Safety padding installation: Fabricating and custom-stitching high-density foam pads with reinforced PVC covers.
  10. Enclosure installation: Fabricating steel uprights, foam sleeves, and fitted safety enclosure mesh.
  11. Mechanical/structural inspection: Verifying weld penetration, structural alignment, and hardware fittings at the factory.
  12. Pre-dispatch inspection: Complete quality check, inventory packaging, and organized logistics dispatch from Nagpur.
  13. Site installation: Assembly of steel frames, spring rigging, mat tensioning, padding layout, and enclosure erection.
  14. Final commissioning: Complete mechanical and safety review, leveling verification, and dynamic bounce checks.
  15. Operator guidance: Training facility operators on daily inspection checklists, operating rules, and routine maintenance procedures.

Trampoline Park vs Bungee Trampoline

Traditional Trampoline Park: Traditional trampoline parks use one or more trampoline surfaces for recreational jumping and activity zones. Jumpers bounce freely using their own momentum across modular stations or interconnected multi-bay arenas without harnesses or mechanical assists.

Bungee Trampoline System: Bungee trampoline systems use harnesses and elastic/bungee systems to assist riders with higher vertical movement, allowing controlled somersaults and elevated bounces under active cord tension.
