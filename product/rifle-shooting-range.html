What is a Rifle Shooting Range?

A Rifle Shooting Range is a controlled recreational or training area designed for target shooting using an appropriate air-rifle setup. The range can include defined shooting lanes, target systems, pellet-catching arrangements, safety partitions, protective backstops and required personal protective equipment.

Starline Adventures customizes air rifle shooting ranges according to:

  • Number of lanes: Scalable multi-lane configurations from 2 to 6+ firing points.
  • Available space: Tailored to fit available indoor recreation halls or outdoor covered pavilions.
  • Target distance: Precision layout planned around the 10-meter standard recreational target line.
  • Indoor/outdoor location: Configured with appropriate ambient shielding or structural weather canopies.
  • Visitor capacity: Sized to manage participant throughput smoothly while maintaining strict firing-line control.
  • Project application: Optimized for commercial resort activities, adventure parks, school sports clubs, or corporate team zones.
  • Site requirements: Built to harmonize with surrounding guest pathways, entry points, and visual lines.

Rifle Shooting Range Design & Engineering

Our engineering approach focuses on creating an orderly, highly disciplined recreation zone where safety and ease of supervision are paramount:

  • Shooting lane layout: Parallel lane orientation with designated firing points and secure rests.
  • Target distance planning: Standard 10-metre target placement with clearly demarcated firing lines.

  • Backstop positioning: Full-coverage ballistic backstops positioned directly behind the target line to arrest stray pellets.
  • Pellet containment: Steel target box design preventing ricochets and capturing spent lead pellets cleanly.
  • Operator/control area: Dedicated supervision station behind the firing line offering an unobstructed view of all lanes.

Air Rifle Shooting Range Setup

Our standard recreational and training setups utilize precision .177 calibre target air rifles designed specifically for competitive and leisure target sports. The complete setup comprises:

  • Defined shooting lanes: Clearly delineated stalls equipped with steadying rests and gun mounts.
  • Target distance: Standard 10-meter line-of-fire from the muzzle plane to the target card face.

Rifle Shooting Range Size & Space Requirement

Because shooting ranges are modular, required space depends on several project-specific variables rather than a single fixed footprint:

  • Number of shooting lanes: Each lane typically requires 3 to 4 feet of comfortable lateral width.
  • Target distance: The shooting corridor typically spans 10 metres (approx. 33 feet) from the firing line to the targets.
  • Lane width: Ample space for participant posture, rifle resting, and coach supervision.
  • Backstop configuration: Additional safety buffer space behind the target boxes for ballistic curtains.
  • Operator area: A minimum of 6 to 8 feet of depth behind the firing line for range officer movement.
  • Entry/exit: Secure queue management pathways keeping waiting visitors safely behind the line.
  • Visitor circulation: Seating, spectator rails, and briefing zones situated well clear of the shooting stalls.
  • Indoor/outdoor site: Structural columns, wall positions, and environmental shielding.
  • Local safety requirements: Site-specific perimeter fencing and clear hazard warning signage.

Rifle Shooting Range Safety System

Safety in an air rifle target range is achieved through rigorous spatial planning, robust physical containment, and disciplined operating procedures:

  • Clearly separated shooting lanes: Solid steel or laminated partitions isolating each shooter's field of view and movement.
  • Appropriate target/backstop arrangement: High-absorption ballistic curtains positioned to reliably catch any high or wide shots.
  • Pellet-catching system: Heavy-gauge steel target traps with angled deflector plates that trap lead pellets without ricochet.
  • Safety partitions: Heavy barrier panels extending past the firing point to prevent barrel sweep toward adjacent lanes.
  • Protective shooting glasses: Mandatory ballistic-rated polycarbonate safety glasses for all shooters and supervisors.
  • Controlled operating area: A single entry gate with a clearly marked red firing-line that no participant crosses during shooting.
  • Proper equipment inspection: Regular checks of air rifle seals, triggers, cocking levers, and barrel alignment.
  • Operator supervision: Strict range commands (e.g., "Load", "Commence Firing", "Cease Fire") enforced by trained staff.
  • Pre-use checks: Daily verification of target box integrity, backstop tension, and clear firing lanes.
  • Regular maintenance: Systematic cleaning, pellet trap emptying, and fastener tightening schedules.
  • Restricted access to the shooting area: Zero-tolerance physical barriers preventing guests from entering downrange territory.
