What is a 360 Degree Cycle?

A 360 Degree Cycle is an adventure ride in which the rider pedals a specially designed cycle through a vertical circular/loop movement. The ride is designed for controlled recreational use and can be customized according to site conditions, rider requirements and project configuration.

In commercial recreation and amusement sectors, this high-thrill attraction is also commonly described as:

  • 360 Degree Inverted Cycle: Referring to the inverted pedaling geometry experienced at the apex of the loop.
  • 360 Degree Cycle Ride: A standalone adventure cycle ride attraction for theme parks and recreation hubs.
  • Inverted Adventure Cycle: An interactive human-powered gravity-challenging thrill ride.

360 Degree Cycle Design & Engineering

The design and engineering of a 360 Degree Cycle require careful technical balance between rider comfort, loop ergonomics, counterbalancing, and structural stability. Key design considerations include:

  • Site-specific design: Adapting base mounting options to indoor flooring or outdoor concrete slabs.
  • Structural configuration: Engineering heavy-gauge circular steel rings to resist rotational dynamics and rider forces.
  • Rider capacity consideration: Sizing counterweights and structural frames for rider weight profiles up to 80 kg.
  • Equipment dimensions: Standard footprint of approximately 6 ft W × 10 ft L × 12 ft H.
  • Mounting/foundation requirements: Chemical or mechanical anchor bolting onto structurally sound concrete surfaces.
  • Track/loop configuration: Circular profile engineered to facilitate smooth, controlled 360-degree inversion cycles.
  • Operational clearance: Establishing safe perimeter buffer zones around moving components and rider trajectory.
  • Custom color and finish: Applying vibrant, weather-resistant powder coat finishes to match project branding.
  • Indoor and outdoor installation planning: Designing for outdoor weather resistance or indoor ceiling clearances.

360 Degree Cycle Safety System

Safety in an inverted cycle ride is governed by appropriate mechanical design, reliable rider securing systems, and disciplined operating procedures. Equipment features and protocols include:

  • Full-body harness: Commercial adjustable full-body harness securely strapping the rider into the cycle seat and frame.
  • Safety helmet: Impact-resistant helmet provided for head protection during active pedaling.
  • Proper rider securing system: Multi-point attachment integrating pedal foot straps and seat restraints to keep riders in position through inversion.
  • Structural inspection: Factory inspection of ring weld joints, circular track curvature, and anchor frame welds.
  • Mechanical inspection: Verification of pivot bearings, rotational axles, chain tension, and wheel attachments.
  • Fastener inspection: Checking high-tension bolts, locking pins, and torque settings prior to commissioning.
  • Counterweight system: Calibrated counterweights engineered to balance rider weight and assist smooth loop completion.
  • Operator-controlled operation: Controlled dispatch with an operator overseeing passenger boarding, harness verification, and ride stopping.
  • Pre-use inspection: Standardized daily checklist covering harness webbing, buckle engagement, bolt tightness, and tire pressure.
  • Regular maintenance: Scheduled lubrication of bearings, track wiping, fastener re-torquing, and harness retirement schedules.

Starline Adventures manufactures each 360 Degree Cycle through a systematic sequence to ensure consistent build quality and mechanical reliability:

  7. Mechanical component installation: Fitting modified bicycle frames, sealed bearings, axles, and counterweights.
  8. Safety equipment integration: Equipping harness anchors, restraint straps, safety helmets, and pedal locks.
  9. Quality inspection: Dimensional checks, balance verification, and weld examination.
  10. Pre-dispatch inspection: Factory operational checks, hardware count, and protective transport packing.
  11. Site installation and commissioning: On-site anchoring, assembly alignment, trial cycles, and client handover.
