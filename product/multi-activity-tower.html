What is a Multi Activity Tower?

A Multi Activity Tower is a multi-level adventure structure designed to accommodate several adventure activities within a single engineered tower. It helps adventure parks, resorts and tourism projects use vertical space efficiently while offering multiple activities from one central structure.

The final activity combination, tower height, number of platforms and capacity can be customized according to the site and project requirements.

Multi Activity Tower Manufacturing Process

Our manufacturing and delivery pipeline follows a systematic, factual engineering workflow:

  1. Requirement discussion: Understanding target guest throughput, site limitations, and desired adventure activities.
  2. Site information collection: Gathering topographical maps, ground dimensions, and soil parameters.
  3. Concept planning: Developing tower layout and activity allocation across different tower faces.
  4. Activity configuration: Finalizing climbing wall, rappelling, zipline, and net climbing placement.
  5. Structural engineering: Calculating column sizing, lattice bracing, wind resistance, and foundation loads.
  6. 3D/model development where applicable: Generating structural 3D models for visualization and spatial clearance checks.
  7. Steel cutting and fabrication: Precision cutting, drilling, and welding of structural mild steel lattice members.
  8. Platform and activity-component fabrication: Fabricating non-slip staging decks, guardrails, and anchor attachments.
  9. Surface protection/finishing: Applying multi-coat anti-corrosive primer and weather-resistant industrial topcoats.
  10. Component inspection: Inspecting structural weld seams, bolt alignments, and component fittings.
  11. Pre-dispatch preparation: Modular bundling, tagging, and secure packing for safe transport.
  12. Site installation: Overseeing foundation interface construction, crane erection of tower tiers, and platform decking attachment.
  13. Final commissioning: Completing activity rigging, lifeline testing, operational checks, and project handover.
