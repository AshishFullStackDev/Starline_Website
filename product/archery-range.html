What is an Archery Range?

An Archery Range is a designated area for target archery where participants shoot arrows toward fixed targets from controlled distances. It provides a structured, safe, and engaging environment for recreational visitors, sports enthusiasts, students, and corporate groups to experience target shooting.

A commercial archery range can include:

  • Shooting lanes: Clearly demarcated shooting channels providing dedicated stance positions for each participant.
  • Target bosses: High-density layered target bosses engineered to absorb and stop arrows safely without damaging arrow shafts.
  • Target stands: Sturdy metallic or timber target support stands positioned securely at designated distances.
  • Safety arrow-stop netting: High-tenacity impact-resistant arrow-stop backstop netting positioned behind and around target lines.
  • Lane separation: Physical lane dividers or spatial markings preventing cross-lane distraction and interference.
  • Bows: Balanced recurve and compound bows suited for commercial recreation and training.
  • Target arrows: Safety fiberglass target arrows paired with blunt or rounded target field points.
  • Arm guards: Protective bracers shielding the inner forearm from bowstring contact.
  • Finger tabs: Ergonomic finger protection tabs or shooting gloves for smooth, safe arrow releases.
  • Operator/supervision area: A dedicated supervision and equipment staging station behind the firing line.

The final configuration depends on the project and site conditions, ensuring an optimal layout that balances guest throughput, visitor circulation, and active range safety.

Archery Range Design & Engineering

The design and layout of an archery range must harmonize participant satisfaction with orderly perimeter control. Our engineering process carefully evaluates key spatial and operational variables:

  • Number of target lanes: Sized from compact 2-lane recreational stations up to expansive 6+ lane activity hubs.
  • Shooting distance: Configured for flexible target distances between 5 metres and 20 metres based on intended user demographics.
  • Lane layout: Parallel lane orientation with designated firing points ensuring all arrows fly in a single uniform direction.
  • Target positioning: Elevated target boss positioning matching eye-level sight lines for ergonomic accuracy.
  • Safety-zone planning: Controlled buffer zones to the sides and rear of the target area to prevent accidental pedestrian ingress.
  • Arrow-stop/backstop arrangement: High-tenacity arrow-stop netting or natural earthen backstops positioned directly behind target lines.
  • Operator area: Supervision space positioned behind the firing line offering an unobstructed view of all active archers.
  • Visitor circulation: Clearly demarcated spectator waiting areas separated by physical handrails or waiting lines.
  • Indoor/outdoor installation: Adaptable structural frames for outdoor resort lawns, covered pavilions, or indoor recreational halls.
  • Site dimensions: Customized footprints engineered to maximize available ground area while preserving required clearance margins.
  • Equipment storage: Secure on-range lockable bow racks, quiver stands, and gear cabinets where required.

Archery Range Size & Space Requirement

Because every commercial recreation site possesses different ground dimensions, there is no single fixed minimum area for an archery range. Instead, required space depends on:

  • Number of target lanes: Each lane requires adequate lateral width for comfortable arm extension and stance stability.
  • Shooting distance: Flexible range lengths between 5 m and 20 m based on selected target configurations.
  • Lane width: Sufficient side clearance between adjacent archers to prevent bumping and distractions.
  • Target size: Boss dimensions matching standard target face sizes.
  • Safety/clearance zones: Buffer zones behind the backstop and along both lateral sides to ensure no bystander crosses into the flight corridor.
  • Arrow-stop arrangement: Sufficient depth behind target stands to hang safety arrow-stop netting with appropriate deceleration drape.
  • Operator area: Clear supervision floor space behind the shooting line for equipment handling, queue control, and coaching.
  • Entry and exit: Dedicated pedestrian access paths that prevent spectators from walking across active shooting paths.
  • Visitor circulation: Safe spectator viewing areas set back behind protective railings.
  • Indoor/outdoor location: Ceiling clearance for indoor setups or perimeter fencing considerations for outdoor grounds.

Archery Range Safety System

Safety in an archery facility is achieved through deliberate spatial organization, robust target containment, participant discipline, and protective equipment. Our archery-specific safety system incorporates:

  • Defined shooting lanes: Clear individual firing points ensuring every participant shoots down a designated linear vector.
  • Controlled shooting direction: Unidirectional shooting toward fixed backstops with strict prohibition of cross-lane firing.
  • Safety arrow-stop netting: Specialized arrow-stopping netting engineered with dense weave and hung with adequate slack to absorb arrow kinetic energy and drop arrows harmlessly.
  • Appropriate target bosses: Layered high-density self-healing foam target bosses that reliably capture arrow points without excessive penetration or bounce-back.
  • Proper target stands: Stable, counterweighted or ground-anchored frames that resist tip-over during arrow impact or wind gusts.
  • Lane separation: Physical dividers or clear safety boundary lines preventing adjacent archers from encroaching on each other's draw space.
  • Arm guards: Durable arm bracers provided to all participants to shield against potential bowstring slap.
  • Finger tabs: Protective tabs or gloves safeguarding archers' fingertips during drawing and release.
  • Suitable target arrows: High-integrity fiberglass target arrows with inspected nocks, straight shafts, and blunt target tips.
  • Operator supervision: Range officers monitoring the firing line, managing arrow retrieval commands, and enforcing range rules.
  • Pre-use equipment inspection: Routine checks of bowstrings, limbs, arrow nocks, and target netting prior to daily operations.
  • Regular maintenance: Periodic rotation of target faces, boss core inspection, and netting tension checks.
  • Controlled access to the shooting area: Gated or cordoned perimeter preventing spectators or animals from entering the flight path while shooting is underway.
